# Story research — real stories, told like a blockbuster

## 1. Hunt (WebSearch, several queries in one turn)
- Angles that win: "survived", "doctors couldn't explain", "the only person who", "trapped for X days", "accidentally discovered",
  "real story behind", "what happens to your body when", "unsolved", "world record", "last seen", "against all odds".
- Sources: major news, science journals/press releases, museum and history sites, court and official reports, well-sourced Wikipedia
  pages (follow their citations). Prefer stories with a clear timeline, a single person at the center and a strange, visual detail.
- Pull 5–8 candidates; score each 1–10 on: hook strength (one shocking sentence?), visual 3D potential, stakes, twist/turn, how unknown it is.

## 2. Verify (never skip)
- Confirm the key facts in 2+ independent sources; note numbers, dates, places exactly.
- Private people: only if they told their story publicly (interviews, their own posts); never invent crimes or claims about real people;
  no gore. Medical/science claims must match the sources. Mark dramatized parts as dramatized in the description.

## 3. Turn it into a short (story-writing + SHORTS RETENTION FORMULA)
- Hook = the strangest true detail, shown in 0–2 s ("He was declared dead… and woke up in the morgue.").
- Compress time ruthlessly; keep the 3 most visual moments as escalations; end on the real twist or the real consequence.
- Second person or the hero's POV, present tense. Add one real number on screen for credibility. The loop line points back to the hook.
- Long-form: one chapter per turning point, a re-hook at each chapter start.

## 4. No real story wanted? Invent one that feels real
- Engines: what-if (one rule of the world changes), inversion (the hunter becomes the hunted), scale (tiny → enormous), consequence chain
  (one small act → 3 escalating effects), countdown (time limit), hidden rule (the clue was on screen).
- Always: one relatable hero, one clear goal, rising danger, a fair twist planted early.

## Output (silent work, then)
Pick the best story yourself; give the user only: the one-line logline and the hook line, then build. Keep sources in the scene's
publish.md (description credits), never as a chat summary.
